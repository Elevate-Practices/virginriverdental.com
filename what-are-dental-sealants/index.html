What Are Dental Sealants and Should Your Child Get Them?

Dental sealants create a protective shield over your child’s molars, blocking food and bacteria from settling into the grooves where cavities commonly form. These thin plastic coatings bond directly to the chewing surfaces of back teeth, offering a proven defense against decay during the years when children are most vulnerable to cavities.

At Virgin River Dental, Dr. Brett Davis and the team help families in St. George protect young smiles with preventive treatments that make a real difference. Dental sealants represent one of the most effective tools available for reducing childhood cavities, and we make the process quick, comfortable, and affordable for every family.

How Dental Sealants Work

Molars contain deep pits and fissures that trap food particles and bacteria, even with regular brushing. These grooves are difficult for toothbrush bristles to reach, creating perfect conditions for decay to develop. Sealants fill these vulnerable spaces with a smooth coating that keeps harmful substances away from tooth enamel.

The application process takes just minutes per tooth. First, we clean and dry the tooth surface thoroughly. Next, we apply an acidic solution that roughens the enamel slightly, helping the sealant bond properly. After rinsing and drying again, we paint the liquid sealant onto the tooth, where it flows into all the grooves and crevices. A special curing light hardens the material in seconds, and the protection is complete.

Once in place, sealants can last several years with normal chewing and eating. Regular dental checkups allow us to monitor their condition and reapply them if needed. The smooth surface also makes teeth easier to clean during daily brushing, adding another layer of cavity prevention.

Benefits of Dental Sealants for Children

Children benefit most from sealants because permanent molars emerge around age 6 and again around age 12. These teeth face immediate exposure to cavity-causing bacteria before kids have fully developed their oral hygiene skills. Protecting these teeth early prevents decay from taking hold during critical growth years.

Studies show that dental sealants reduce cavities significantly in the years following application. Even as the protection decreases over time, sealed teeth still experience fewer cavities than untreated teeth. This prevention saves families from more extensive and costly treatments like fillings or crowns later.

The procedure involves no drilling, no discomfort, and no recovery time. Kids can eat and drink normally right after the appointment. This makes sealants an ideal preventive measure for children who may feel anxious about dental work. The investment in sealants often costs less than treating a single cavity.

When Should Your Child Get Sealants

The ideal time for sealants is as soon as permanent molars appear. First molars typically emerge around age 6, while second molars come in around age 12. Applying sealants shortly after these teeth break through the gums offers the best protection during the years when cavities develop most rapidly.

Some children may also benefit from sealants on baby teeth if those teeth have deep grooves or if the child has a history of cavities. Protecting primary molars helps ensure they remain healthy until permanent teeth are ready to replace them. We evaluate each child’s individual risk factors and recommend sealants based on their specific needs.

Children who already have cavities in their molars cannot receive sealants on those teeth. However, we can still seal healthy teeth to prevent additional decay. Early dental visits help us identify the right timing for sealant application and catch any developing problems before they require more involved treatment.

Are Dental Sealants Right for Your Family

Most children make excellent candidates for dental sealants, especially those with deep grooves in their molars or a history of cavities. Kids who struggle with thorough brushing, consume sugary foods frequently, or have difficulty reaching their back teeth during cleaning particularly benefit from this extra protection.

Cost considerations often concern parents, but sealants represent a smart investment in long-term oral health. Many dental insurance plans cover sealants for children, and the expense remains minimal compared to treating cavities. We accept major PPO insurance plans and offer flexible payment options to make preventive care accessible.

The decision ultimately depends on your child’s individual risk factors and oral health needs. During regular cleanings and exams, we assess tooth anatomy, cavity history, and oral hygiene habits to determine whether sealants would provide meaningful protection. We also discuss your concerns and preferences to ensure you feel confident in the treatment plan.
